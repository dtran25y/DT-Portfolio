Escape Flood 3 :

Made in: Unity
Production Time: 3 Weeks
Role: Graphics / VFX / Gameplay Programmer

Project Outline:


This "personal" project is a timed 3D platformer that is meant to challenge the player to go faster, as water rises from the ground up, where you must reach the highest point in the level in order to not drown.

The overall project is meant to build a stronger portfolio (with one Teacher-led project and one project of my own) that will help prepare me for the industry by researching jobs and the kinds of skills they require in their description.

"Escape Flood 3" is a mini-project that is built in mind with developing the skills I lack strength in. Therefore, the week prior to production was spent looking into the job market, until I started ideating my personal project during production which was based on my own research into the job market and what kind of role I wanted to get into from the gaming industry, which was a blend of gameplay programming and graphics programming as well, using node graphs and HLSL.


Career Research:

This research I conducted is detailing the 3 skills I want to develop throughout my mini-project which are the following: Shading & Lighting with GPU-based Shaders, VFX Work in 3D, and working with a different engine or working with a different port such as mobile.

So with my MVP, I envisioned a player with more advanced movement scripts than usual such as diving or rolling, in addition to working on realistic water shaders and grass to show proven work of graphics programming. As for VFXs, I intended to create custom effects and particles for the player to deduce their movement such as dust trails and such to enhance the feel and visual clarity of gameplay to player.

I ultimately decided that it would be better to work on the first two skills as they coincide more intristically with the GPU rendering pipeline and felt much more realstic to complete this mini-project of 3 weeks instead of trying to cram every improvement I possibly need and call it quits. This allowed me to focus more heavily of learning and getting to use Unity's particle system (which I had used previously in few instances), HLSL, shader graphs, and VFX graphs and get a grasp on these tools for future projects.

As for my third skill (working with different languages and engines / porting to different platforms), it was something I have to consider in another project as trying to learn rendering with Shaders and VFX with an unfamiliar game engine would be more unecessary confusion and labour on myself. This I'd also have to understand their workspace and architecture, as I still choose to work in Unity for the time-being until I'm willing to explore other engines such as Unreal.

Production:


After the career research, I went ahead and started working on the player and most important features for the first week such as the movement, camera and rising-water Object.

The player movement relies on character controller component, which doesn't use full physics simulations such as rigidbodies. This allowed me to have complete control over the player in terms of velocity, gravity, and everything to do with movement, without being completely affected by any external forces such as how a rigidbody would interact.

This makes it great for my timed 3D platformer, as having much more fluid movement that is less likely to feel interrupted is something I think is essential to the concept. However, this meant that I had to code my own logic and physics to the player with advanced movements in mind. I managed to get most of the functions full working, yet I still had to cut crouching and rolling from my game to save on time due to the other tasks at hand. Still, this part of the mini-project was a great chance to put my problem-solving skills at the test, as it really challenged my ability as a mathmetician in terms of how I could possibly simulate the player physics which strengthen my programming skills overall.

Then I went ahead and worked on the shaders for the water that would rise and drown the player if beneath it. The script itself was easy, making the 'waterObject' translate up with a speed calculated to how mcuh time should be given for each level, and if the palyer falls beneath a certain Y-level under the water (e.g. the player object transform.y is 5.0f lower than the water), the player would be deleted and restart the level.

I would have definetely made this a much more sophisticated mechanic such as drowning and giving the player a visible health / oxygen meter, however with the time I had this would be a less concerning goal in mind of the main purpose of this project (to learn how to create VFX and Shaders).

The most challenging aspect would be having to learn GPU Rendering Pipeline on Unity itself, which took quite a while to get a grasp on and cut back on most features I wanted like custom VFX for the players mobility, especially with HLSL being a difficult task to learn as a whole new language to add, despite it's familiar syntax to ones I already know.

The water shader was both inspired by my influence and was overall designed around recreating a believably realstic creation of water, which was created with node graphs.
